## Section 3 — source quotes (the war stories behind each question)

**#1 · Bad Process Design Before Implementation**
> "Number one — and this is the biggest killer — most companies try to make Dynamics Field Service fit their broken processes instead of fixing their processes first. They bring in the software hoping it will fix their operations. That almost never works."

**#2 · Underestimating Change Management**
> "Number two is change management. Field technicians and dispatchers are some of the most resistant groups to new technology you will ever meet. If you don't properly train them and get their buy-in, they will find ways to work around the system. I've seen it dozens of times."

**#3 · The Scheduling Trap**
> "Number three is scheduling. Companies rush to turn on Resource Scheduling Optimization way too early. RSO is powerful, but if your data and processes aren't rock solid first, it will make your problems worse, not better."

**#4 · Garbage In, Garbage Out**
> "Number four is data quality. Most companies underestimate how bad their data actually is. You put bad data into Field Service, you're going to get bad schedules, bad recommendations, and bad results."

**#5 · Choosing the Wrong Partner**
> "Number five — and this one hurts — a lot of companies choose the wrong implementation partner. Just because someone is a Microsoft Gold Partner doesn't mean they actually know Field Service deeply."

## Section 4 — The Warning Signs You're in Trouble (11:00–12:30)
> "If you're currently in a Field Service implementation, here are the four warning signs you're in danger:
> 1. Your project team keeps saying 'we'll figure that out later'
> 2. The consultants don't seem to understand the day-to-day reality of your technicians
> 3. Leadership keeps pushing to go live even though the team isn't ready
> 4. You're spending more time configuring the system than improving your actual processes
>
> If you're seeing any of these… you need to pump the brakes."
